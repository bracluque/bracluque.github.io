**Abstract:**
> This pilot study aimed at developing and validating an evaluation model to assess the impact of participation in the National History Day in Ohio program (NHD-OH) on students academic achievement and intellectual growth by using as key indicators: A) High-stakes tests, i.e., Ohio Graduation Tests (OGT) in Social Studies, Reading and Writing; B) Appreciation of history, and C) Development of critical thinking. Using a quasi-experimental study design, twenty-four tenth-graders participating in NHD-OH and twenty-four matched controls were used in the analysis to reduce the impact of selection bias. As a result of this study it was found that students participating in NHD-OH received statistically significant higher scores on the Social Studies OGT, Reading and Writing; and furthermore, demonstrated a deeper appreciation of history and a better ability to explore and integrate new perspectives.

### 12b. Linear prose form *(copy-paste friendly)*

> Data were collected using the Ohio Graduation Test (OGT) in Social Studies, Reading, and Writing, which are high-stakes state standardized assessments administered to all 10th graders in Ohio. Prior reading achievement was measured using the Ohio Achievement Test (OAT) taken in 8th grade, used as a baseline for propensity score matching. Appreciation of history was assessed through a researcher-designed questionnaire with open- and closed-ended questions exploring attitudes toward history in the classroom and everyday life. Development of critical thinking was evaluated through focus group discussions using a rubric adapted from Garrison's (1991) five-stage conceptual model, with inter-rater reliability of 78%.

## 14. Notes

### 14a. Bulleted notes
- Very small sample: 24 treatment and 24 matched control students (48 total). Meets the minimum threshold of 20 per group but just barely.
- Propensity score matching was used based on gender, ethnicity, need for social service benefits, and previous OAT reading score.
- The paper reports Student's t-test p-values but not t-statistics or standard errors in the main table (Table 1).
- OAT Reading baseline scores showed no significant difference between groups (p = 0.139), supporting baseline equivalence.
- The study is a pilot evaluation project; the design was intended to be replicated at larger scale.
- The program is co-curricular (yearlong enrichment), not strictly an extended school day, but involves extended educational engagement beyond regular classes.
- The study is in the United States (not a developing/LAC country).
- Critical thinking was assessed qualitatively via focus groups, not quantitatively.

### 14b. Linear prose summary *(copy-paste friendly)*

> This pilot study uses a quasi-experimental design with propensity score matching to evaluate the National History Day in Ohio (NHD-OH) program among 48 tenth-grade students (24 treatment, 24 matched controls) from Columbus and Dayton urban public school districts. The sample is at the minimum threshold for inclusion. The paper reports means, SDs, and p-values from Student's t-tests but does not report t-statistics or standard errors. The study finds significant differences on OGT Writing (p = 0.000), OGT Reading (p = 0.018), and OGT Social Studies (p = 0.002), with no significant baseline difference in OAT Reading (p = 0.139). The program is co-curricular rather than a traditional extended school day intervention. The study is in the United States, not a developing or LAC country.
